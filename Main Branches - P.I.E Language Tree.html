Main Branches

Germanic

The Germanic branch is the largest of the three main branches of the Indo-European language family. Germanic is the most spoken branch of the Indo-European language family, largely due to English becoming the global lingua franca. Originating somewhere in Denmark, the Germanic languages diverged through phonetic shifts known as Grimm’s and Verner’s Laws (for example, Latin “pater” in comparison to English “father”). English, Scots, German, Luxembourgish, Afrikaans, Dutch, Swedish, Danish, Norwegian, Icelandic

Italic (Romance)

The Italic branch, also known as Romance languages, evolved from Latin, the language of the Roman Empire. As the empire expanded, Latin spread across Europe, eventually diverging into distinct languages like Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, and Romanian. These languages share a common vocabulary and grammar due to their Latin roots. Languages: Latin, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese, Romansh, Romagnol, Romanian, Catalan

Balto-Slavic

Due to the similar traits in vocabulary, phonetics, and grammar, the Baltic and Slavic languages are usually grouped together. The Slavic branch is the majority, containing Russian, Bulgarian, etc. Meanwhile Baltic comprises of Lithuanian and Latvian. Lithuanian is the most conservative living Indo-European language, keeping many linguistic features lost to time, yet is proven useful in reconstructing PIE. Languages: Latvian, Lithuanian, Russian, Belarusian, Polish, Czech, Slovak, Slovene, Macedonian, Serbo-Croatian, Ukrainian, Bulgarian

Indo-Iranian

The biggest branch and THE most diverse of the Indo-European family. Over HUNDREDS of these languages are grouped into the Indo-Aryan and Iranian sub-branches. This branch is widespread within South Asia, from the ouskirts of Iranian plateau, to the Indian Subcontinent, to within the islands of Sri Lanka and the Maldives. The largest of these languages is Hindi, which, along with Urdu, is part of the pluricentral language called Hindustani.
